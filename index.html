Emergency Phone Triage Checklist (What to tell the vet receptionist):

  • 1. Exact Weight Loss: State the precise weight drop in grams (e.g., *"He has dropped 55g in 4 days"*).
  • 2. Eating & Pooping Status: Confirm whether they are eating hay independently and whether droppings have stopped or shrunk.
  • 3. Ask for an Exotic Specialist: Explicitly ask: *"Do you have a vet on duty today who regularly treats guinea pigs and dental malocclusion?"*
  • 4. Antibiotic Safety Check: Be prepared to remind them that penicillin-based drugs are fatal to cavies if any antibiotics are prescribed.

Weekly 8-Point Physical Health Check-Up

Perform this structured check weekly. Click each point to read detailed assessment guidance and troubleshooting tips.

1. Incisors & Dry Chin

What to check: Front incisors should meet evenly without overlapping, misaligning, or sharp spurs. The chin must be completely dry.

Troubleshooting: Wet chin or drooling indicates dental malocclusion (overgrown back molars) or mouth pain making swallowing difficult. Book an exotic vet appointment immediately.

2. Silent Breathing

What to check: Hold your piggy gently near your ear. Respiration should be effortless and completely silent.

Troubleshooting: Clicking, rattling, wheezing, or wet sounding breaths indicate an Upper Respiratory Infection (URI) or pneumonia, which can deteriorate rapidly in guinea pigs.

3. Clear Eyes & Nose

What to check: Eyes must be bright, bold, and clear with zero cloudiness or milky discharge. Nose must be clean and free of crust.

Troubleshooting: Milky eye discharge can signal eye injuries or respiratory flare-ups. Crusty nostrils point toward infection or allergies to dusty hay.

4. Plentiful Droppings

What to check: Normal poops are consistent, firm, oval-shaped pellets produced in high volume throughout the day.

Troubleshooting: Small, misshapen, or clustered poops indicate gut stasis or lack of fibre intake. Watery diarrhoea is an emergency requiring vet intervention.

5. Clean Footpads

What to check: Inspect footpads for smooth skin and absence of swelling, redness, or scabs (pododermatitis / bumblefoot).

Troubleshooting: Bumblefoot is caused by damp bedding or abrasive wire flooring. Switch to clean fleece liners or absorbent paper bedding and consult a vet if sores appear.

6. Coat & Skin Check

What to check: Part the fur to inspect skin health. Look for dry flakes, scabs, bald spots, or excessive scratching.

Troubleshooting: Flaking skin, hair loss in a V-shape, or intense scratching often point towards burrowing mites (mange mites) or fungal ringworm, which require vet-prescribed treatments.

7. Dry Bottom & Urine

What to check: Rear end must be completely dry and clean. Boars require checks for impaction (blockage in anal sac).

Troubleshooting: Wet fur around the tail can mean urinary tract infections, sludge, or impaction in older boars. Clean gently and seek vet advice if odour or pain occurs.

8. Grease Gland Maintenance

What to check: Located on the rump just above the tail. Boars in particular secrete waxy oils that can accumulate.

Troubleshooting: Clean periodically with a tiny dab of coconut oil or mild pet shampoo to dissolve waxy buildup, then rinse thoroughly to prevent skin irritation.

What is an Exotic Vet?

An exotic vet is a specialist veterinary surgeon with advanced training in treating non-traditional pets like guinea pigs, rabbits, and small mammals, rather than just standard cats and dogs.

Why guinea pigs specifically need one:
  • Specialised Dental Care: Guinea pig teeth grow continuously. Standard vets often lack the specific tools needed to properly inspect and burr back molar spurs or malocclusion.
  • Fatal Drug Sensitivities: Many standard antibiotics prescribed to dogs and cats (like penicillin-based drugs) are completely fatal to guinea pigs. Exotic vets know safe alternatives like Baytril or Septrin.
  • Masking Illness: As prey animals, guinea pigs hide pain until they are very sick. Exotic specialists are trained to spot subtle clinical signs that standard vets might miss.
